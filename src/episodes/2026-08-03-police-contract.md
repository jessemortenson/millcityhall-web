## Minneapolis For a Better Police Contract

This advocacy group ([website](https://www.mplsforabetterpolicecontract.org)) was formed by members who included the Racial Justice Network and Communities United Against Police Brutality. They initially put out 19 recommendations for changes to the contract way back in 2019. They have also sought to win the support of city councilmembers for those proposals. Unfortunately, they were not enacted. In 2022, those were expanded to [22 recommendations](https://www.mplsforabetterpolicecontract.org/recommendations-1), and ultimately four councilmembers voted against the 2023-2025 contract. 

In our conversation, we referred to this group as pretty tightly associated with Michelle Gross/Communities United Against Police Brutaility. Stacey Gurian-Sherman, a steering committee member for MFBPC, helped clarify that this has always been an independent group, not a project "of" CUAPB. Gross and Dave Bicking had left MFBPC earlier than we indicated in our conversation.

## 2023-2025 Contract

- 21% pay raise
- Federation approved in 301-63 vote
- Sept 2023: negotiations started
- July 2024, city council approves new contract

### Media coverage

- [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minneapolis-police-department-contract-vote/)
- [Associated Press](https://www.police1.com/police-recruitment/minneapolis-approves-new-pd-contract-that-will-raise-officer-pay-by-22)
- [Kare 11](https://www.kare11.com/article/news/local/tentative-mpd-contract-comes-with-double-digit-raises-new-reforms/89-9733a2d2-6989-4404-96f2-70a9a47cf746)
  - The new agreement will give management more discretion over job assignments and staffing ratios, so that the Chief and his leadership team can assign officers to areas of the greatest need, and make promotions based on candidate readiness, not based upon a mandated percentage.
  - The City also negotiated a change to significantly reduce the period of time it takes to fill vacancies.
  - Through the new agreement, the POFM has agreed that the City can make permanent hires or hire civilians to assist with investigative work.
  - Officers will no longer receive automatic notification of the identity of the person requesting public personnel data about them.
  - When there are allegations of severe misconduct, the Chief will have complete discretion to place the officer on paid investigatory leave for up to 180 days. In the past, the Chief only had complete discretion for the first 30 days.
