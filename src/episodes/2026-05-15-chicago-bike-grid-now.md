## What is the transformational power of a systemic approach?

We know that it takes truly safe and comfortable infrastructure to make biking and walking viable as a regular transportation option for most people. It takes infrastructure that prioritizes the safety and comfort of pedestrians, bicyclists, and other folks engaging in active transportation.

But in the United States, we almost always encounter such infrastructure in isolated and disconnected parts. We encounter a really nice bike trail or a protected bike lane, but it might not connect to any other similar routes. The lack of a system itself is a barrier. 

We know from examples around the globe that, when infastructure is both safe AND systemic, active transportation becomes a popular option. Whether for children's safety and health, for the enrichment of communal and commercial life, for making housing more affordable, or for [fighting global warming](https://arstechnica.com/cars/2023/11/280-million-e-bikes-are-slashing-oil-demand-far-more-than-electric-vehicles/): the benefits of active transportation are enormous. And the costs of car-dependency are huge.

* [Bogotá, Colombia was an early pioneer](https://itdp.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/From-Transmilenio-to-Cycle-Networks-Lessons-Learned-from-Bogotas-Comprehensive-Urban-Mobility-Planning-MAY4.pdf) (PDF)
* [Amsterdam, the Netherlands is an often cited example](https://www.theguardian.com/cities/2015/may/05/amsterdam-bicycle-capital-world-transport-cycling-kindermoord), but not as many know that this is the result of a transformation of a very car-centric system
* [Paris, France](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/05/how-paris-swapped-cars-for-bikes-and-remade-its-streets) very recently made huge strides towards a safe grid under mayor Anne Hidalgo
