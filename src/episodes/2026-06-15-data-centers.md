As anger at the Big Tech data center boom seems to be boiling across layers of society, data center politics landed in Minneapolis last month. The City Council passed a temporary moratorium - with generous exceptions - on new data centers in the city. We look at some of the data center projects that exist today, and those that appear to be in-progress. How do we think about hyperscale? We also examine the downtown vacancy dilemma, and the challenges and tradeoffs between data center conversions and housing conversions. When and how do data centers make sense in Minneapolis?

Recorded 6/10/26.

## Data Centers

### City Data Center Moratorium

- Six month moratorium
- Downtown data centers under 350,000 square feet exempted
- Study will commence to review possible changes to ordinance/zoning code
- 8-5 vote

## What About Converting To Residential?

### Twin Cities Support for Residential Conversion

- 2024: [Council Members Cashman and Rainville](https://lims.minneapolismn.gov/file/2024-00705) successfully pushed two changes to reduce developer barriers to residential conversions ([Star Tribune coverage](https://www.startribune.com/downtown-minneapolis-law-office-building-residential-housing-conversion/601151277))
- 2025: Saint Paul Mayor Carter promoted changes to induce residential conversion in his [proposed 2025 budget](https://web.archive.org/web/20250423182038/https://www.stpaul.gov/departments/mayors-office/2025-budget-address)
- 2026: Mayor Frey re-iterated that change and his support for residential conversion [in his state of the city speech](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/government/mayor/speeches/2026-state-of-the-city/)
- 2026: Attempt to create a significant tax credit for residential conversion [re-introduced in the MN legislature](https://pluralpolicy.com/app/legislative-tracking/bill/details/state-mn-2025_2026-sf768/2542241), but does not pass. ([Star Tribune mention](https://www.startribune.com/downtown-minneapolis-st-paul-office-building-housing-conversion-save-cost/601172348))

[Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/stella-landmark-tower-hamm-building-114000787.html)

Paul Downtown Alliance study identified another 10 buildings as candidates [web archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20250506150223/https://downtownstpaul.com/saint-paul-downtown-alliance-announces-office-to-residential-conversion-study-findings-shares-six-month-downtown-investment-strategy-progress/)

## Downtown Vacancy

- Q4 2025: The office vacancy rate in downtown Minneapolis sits at 32.6%, [Twin Cities Business Magazine](https://tcbmag.com/pros-cons-and-complexities-of-downtown-data-centers/)
- Sept 2024: "The overall vacancy rate in Minneapolis’ central business district at the end of June was 33%, exceeding the metro-wide average, according to a quarterly report from Cushman and Wakefield" [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/downtown-minneapolis-law-office-building-residential-housing-conversion/601151277)
- Office valuations continue to drop. Downtown Minneapolis’ commercial real estate values fell 13.7% last year, according to the city’s assessment report [City of Minneapolis](https://lims.minneapolismn.gov/Download/Agenda/8614/5882/2026AssessmentReport.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Oct 2024: "Office vacancies in the core of downtown Minneapolis reached a new high this fall " [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/downtown-minneapolis-office-building-vacancy-value-sale-price/601159968)
  - By the end of September, the average office vacancy rate in the central business district (CBD) of downtown rose slightly to 23.4%, according to a quarterly report from Colliers. The situation was even worse in downtown St. Paul, which saw a 29% vacancy rate, while the combined suburbs rose to 11.5%.
  - The Dayton’s Project, the much-heralded $350 million redevelopment of the former department store on Nicollet Mall, went into receivership last month.
  - Some new developments:
    - the Kickernick Building, which recently opened an art gallery. Earlier this year, Twin Cities-based United Properties sold the historic former warehouse on the edge of the CBD for $3.79 million. In 2017, United paid $19.5 million for the building
    -  Tom McCarver and Steve Boynton bought a mixed-use, nearly 31,000-square-foot building at the corner of Seventh Street and Hennepin Avenue that most recently housed Seven Steakhouse & Sushi. Last month, they paid about $4.3 million, slightly more than half of what it sold for in November 2017.
